Privacy Policy

Last updated: June 29, 2026

1. Introduction

SOLace ("we," "our," or "us") is committed to protecting your privacy. This Privacy Policy explains how SOLace collects, uses, and discloses information when you use our educational testing platform. SOLace complies with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), including the amended COPPA Rule that took effect April 22, 2026.

2. Information We Collect

2.1 Information Collection by Age Group

Our information collection practices apply the same protections to all users regardless of age, because our platform collects no personally identifying information from anyone under any circumstances.

2.2 Information We Do Not Collect

We do NOT collect any personally identifying information from any user, including:

  • Names, usernames, or display names
  • Email addresses or contact information (unless provided to us voluntarily by a user over the age of 13 through a third-party system)
  • Photos or images
  • Location information (beyond country, which may be inferred by our hosting provider for security and infrastructure purposes only)
  • Device identifiers that could identify a specific user
  • Persistent browser identifiers such as cookies or fingerprints that could track a user across sessions
  • Any other personal information as defined by COPPA

2.3 Anonymous Performance Data

SOLace collects anonymous performance data from completed test attempts to support platform improvement and educational research. This data contains no personally identifying information. The specific fields collected are:

  • The subject area and specific test taken
  • Overall score and total time spent on the test
  • Per-question data: whether each question was answered correctly, time spent on each question, and the number of times the answer was changed before submission
  • Timestamps for when the test started and was completed

Each test attempt is tagged with a random session identifier generated by the student's browser at the start of the test. The identifier exists only in the browser's memory during the test session and is discarded when the tab is closed. We do not store cookies, account information, device fingerprints, or any other persistent identifier that could link test attempts to individual users, sessions, or devices. We do not store IP addresses with this data.

Because no persistent identifier connects one attempt to another, a student who takes multiple tests produces multiple unlinked records from our perspective. This architecture prevents any reconstruction of an individual student's history even from within our own database.

3. How We Use Information

We use the anonymous performance data described in Section 2.3 to:

  • Provide, maintain, and improve our Service
  • Identify questions or content areas where students consistently struggle, so we can refine explanations, hint quality, and question design
  • Track aggregate usage patterns and trends across subjects, grade bands, and question types
  • Measure the effectiveness of new features and content additions
  • Support peer-reviewed educational research through qualified academic partners under the conditions described in Section 4.3
  • Detect, investigate, and prevent fraudulent requests and other illegal activities

Although your activity contributes to our larger pool of anonymous data, no identifiable information is associated with your device, session, or visit at any point.

4. How We Share Information

We may share information in the following circumstances:

4.1 Service Providers

We share only the information you voluntarily provide to us with third-party service providers who perform services on our behalf such as email delivery. Our database provider (currently Neon, a serverless Postgres service) hosts the anonymous performance data described in Section 2.3 on secure, access-controlled infrastructure. Our database provider operates under its own privacy and security obligations and does not use the data for any purpose other than providing storage services to us.

4.2 Legal Requirements

We may disclose information if required to do so by law or in response to valid requests by public authorities.

4.3 Academic Research Partners

We may share anonymous, aggregate performance data with qualified academic researchers at accredited educational institutions. Any such sharing takes place under formal data-sharing agreements that limit use to the stated research purpose, prohibit attempts at re-identification, and prohibit onward disclosure. Because no personally identifying information exists in the data at any stage, such sharing does not involve the transfer of personal information as defined by COPPA. Where appropriate, research projects using this data receive prior review by the partner institution's Institutional Review Board (IRB).

5. Data Retention

SOLace does not retain personal information from any user, because such information is not collected other than voluntary contact information from users over 13 as noted above.

Anonymous performance data described in Section 2.3 is retained for up to 24 months from the date of collection. After this period, records are automatically deleted from our database. This retention window reflects the time needed to support meaningful longitudinal analysis while limiting the data footprint in line with COPPA's data minimization principles.

Any educational progress or scores displayed back to a student on their own device are stored locally in that browser and are independent of the anonymous data sent to our servers. Clearing browser storage removes the student's local history without affecting the anonymous data already collected, and vice versa.

6. Cookies and Similar Technologies

We do not use cookies or similar tracking technologies to collect personal information from users of any age. Any local storage used by SOLace is strictly functional (such as maintaining accessibility preferences or a student's own history of prior test scores on their own device) and does not collect or transmit personal information to our servers.

6.1 Parental Control

Parents can control local storage settings in their child's browser to further limit any local data retention.

7. Children's Privacy (COPPA Compliance)

SOLace is designed as an educational platform suitable for users of all ages, including children under 13. We comply with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and the amended COPPA Rule effective April 22, 2026.

7.1 Information Collection from Children Under 13

We do not knowingly collect, use, or disclose personal information from children under the age of 13 without verifiable parental consent. Our practices for users under 13 include:

  • No Account Registration: Children under 13 can use SOLace without creating an account
  • No Personal Information Collection: We do not collect names, email addresses, phone numbers, or other personal identifiers from users under 13
  • Anonymous Performance Data Only: Test performance data sent to our servers contains no personally identifying information. Each test session is tagged with a random identifier that exists only in the browser's memory during the test and is discarded when the tab closes. No cookies, accounts, or device fingerprints link test attempts to individual students. See Section 2.3 for the complete list of fields collected.
  • No Behavioral Tracking: We do not use cookies or similar technologies to track the online activities of users under 13 across sessions, across devices, or across websites
  • No Advertising: We do not display advertisements of any kind, and we do not use any data for advertising purposes

7.2 Third-Party Data Collection

SOLace contains links to third-party websites. Websites that are COPPA-compliant and safe for users under 13 to access, such as Desmos, are available for all users through our site by default. Clicking a third-party link to a website designed for users over 13, such as GitHub, triggers a browser confirmation asking the user to confirm they are over 13 and wish to access the website. SOLace is not responsible for any privacy violations that occur within third-party websites we link to.

7.3 Parental Rights

Parents and legal guardians of children under 13 have the right to:

  • Review any personal information we may have collected about their child (though we do not collect such information)
  • Request that we delete any information associated with their child's use of the platform
  • Refuse to allow any further collection or use of information related to their child's use of the platform
  • Contact us with questions about our privacy practices regarding children

Because we do not collect personal information and our anonymous performance data contains no linkable identifier, we cannot selectively locate or delete records associated with a particular child. Parents concerned about the anonymous data collection can prevent it entirely by closing the browser tab before a test completes, as the data is transmitted only upon completion.

7.4 Parental Consent

If we ever change our practices to collect personal information from children under 13, we commit to obtaining verifiable parental consent before collecting any such information, as required by COPPA.

7.5 Contact for Child Privacy Concerns

If you are a parent or guardian and have concerns about your child's privacy or believe your child has provided personal information to us, please contact us immediately at support@learnsolace.org with "Child Privacy" in the subject line.

8. International Data Transfers

Your information may be transferred to and maintained on servers located outside of your jurisdiction where privacy laws may differ. By using our Service, you consent to such transfers. Because no personally identifying information is collected or transferred, the privacy implications of any such transfer are minimal.

9. Teacher Class Feature

SOLace offers an optional class management feature that allows teachers to organize their students into named classes and track aggregate test performance by seat. This section describes the privacy model that governs the class feature.

9.1 Seat-Token Model — No Student Names on Our Servers

SOLace servers store no student names at any point. Within the class feature, each student is represented by an opaque seat token (for example, "Seat 14") — a neutral label that carries no personally identifying information. The mapping between a seat token and a student's real name exists only in the teacher's own browser, stored in that browser's local storage. This name-to-token map is never transmitted to SOLace in any form.

Concretely:

  • The SOLace database contains no column for student first name, last name, or any other student name field in any table
  • No SOLace network request ever carries a student name in its body, query string, or headers
  • A student who joins a class and submits tests is represented to our servers only as a seat token identifier and the anonymous performance data described in Section 2.3
  • If a teacher enters student names on their device to associate names with seats, those names are stored only in that teacher's browser local storage, under the teacher's own control

9.2 Teacher Authentication

Teachers who use the class management feature create a password-protected account using their email address. Teacher accounts are subject to the following practices:

  • Passwords are hashed using the argon2id algorithm with parameters exceeding OWASP recommendations; the raw password is never stored
  • Email addresses are used only to authenticate the teacher and send account-related notices (email verification, password reset). Teacher email addresses are not shared with students or used for marketing
  • Session tokens ride in httpOnly, Secure cookies and are never stored in browser local storage
  • Teachers may optionally enable two-factor authentication (TOTP) for additional account security

Students do not create accounts and do not authenticate. A student joins a class by entering a join code provided by their teacher and selecting their seat from a list of seat labels. No student credentials of any kind are created or stored.

9.3 Local Roster File — Teacher Responsibility

If a teacher chooses to associate student names with seat tokens, those names exist only in the teacher's browser local storage and in any roster file the teacher exports to their device. SOLace has no access to, and no copy of, that local name data. The security and confidentiality of local roster information is entirely the teacher's responsibility. Teachers are encouraged to handle exported roster files with the same care as any document containing student names, and to delete them when they are no longer needed.

9.4 Class Data Retention

Anonymous performance data linked to a class seat token is retained for 365 days after the associated class is archived or deleted, after which it is automatically removed. This retention period gives teachers a reasonable window to review historical performance data before it is purged. Anonymous test attempts that are not linked to any class (the standard anonymous path) are retained for up to 24 months as described in Section 5.

Because SOLace stores no student names, there is no name data to delete. The underlying seat-token performance records contain no information from which a student's identity could be recovered.

10. Local Storage and the Class Feature

The class feature uses browser local storage on the teacher's device to maintain the seat-to-name roster map and on the student's device to remember which class and seat the student has joined. The following local storage keys may be present:

  • Teacher device: A roster map keyed by class ID, containing seat token IDs mapped to first and last names as entered by the teacher. This data never leaves the teacher's browser.
  • Student device: The class ID, seat token ID, class name, and seat label for the class the student most recently joined. This data contains no student name. A student can clear it at any time by clicking "Join different class" on any test page, or by clearing browser storage.

Neither of these local storage entries is transmitted to SOLace servers. They exist solely to provide continuity across page loads on the respective device.
